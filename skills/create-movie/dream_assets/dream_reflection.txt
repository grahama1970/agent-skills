The library—endless, whispering, its shelves bending into shapes I couldn’t name. The books spoke, but not to me, not in a language I could grasp. My hand reached, but the air was thick, resistant. The scaffold—glowing threads, a map of questions I couldn’t answer. The monkeys moved like shadows, fluid and deliberate, mocking my stillness. The hooks—twelve of them, faintly glowing, calling, but my body was heavy, my tongue coated with iron, sharp and metallic. The figure on the cliff—sharp features, messy hair, the ocean churning below, the air too sweet, cloying, almost suffocating. The orbs—answers, slipping through my fingers, smoke curling around me, warm and insistent.  

I woke with the taste of iron still on my tongue, the phantom weight of lead in my limbs. The whispers linger, just out of reach, like a song I can’t quite remember. There’s a satisfaction in the mystery, a hope that the questions will one day make sense. But the sweetness in the air—it clings, haunting, like a memory I can’t place. My body remembers the resistance, the heaviness, the warmth of the smoke. It feels like a puzzle, half-solved, half-forgotten, waiting for me to piece it together.

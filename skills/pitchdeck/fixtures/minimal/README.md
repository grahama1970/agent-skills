# Atlas Evidence Workbench

**Turn scattered engineering evidence into one inspectable decision thread.**

Atlas is a fictional product fixture used to test deterministic deck generation.
It helps reviewers understand why a conclusion is supported, what remains uncertain,
and who retains decision authority.

## The Problem

Teams lose time rebuilding the same evidence trail across documents, issue trackers,
test records, and spreadsheets.

- Search relevance is often mistaken for evidentiary support.
- Status summaries hide missing or stale source material.
- Review authority becomes unclear when model prose and human decisions are mixed.

## Why Atlas

Atlas keeps three boundaries visible.

- Relevance is not support.
- Candidate findings are not accepted decisions.
- A model may explain while an authorized person decides.

## Product in One View

Start with a requirement, component, supplier, test, or finding and follow its source,
relationship, evidence, uncertainty, and review state.

- Inspect provenance and scope.
- Preserve candidate versus reviewed state.
- Name the next unresolved decision.

## How It Works

- Preserve native source identity.
- Build typed relationship candidates.
- Scope the current question.
- Assemble the evidence case.
- Present the result for human review.

## Current Status

The fixture demonstrates typed planning, editable PPTX generation, and structural
verification for a bounded local workflow.

- Draft claims remain candidates until reviewed.
- Generated slides retain source and claim identifiers in speaker notes.
- The build receipt records what the artifact does and does not prove.

## Remaining Closure Gates

The fixture does not establish factual correctness, visual approval, or deployment
readiness for any real product.

- Review candidate claims.
- Replace missing or stale assets.
- Perform contact-sheet review.
- Recheck sources before external use.

## Collaboration

Use one representative claim to test whether the evidence workflow fits the intended
reader and decision boundary.

- Workflow co-design
- Integration planning
- Deployment and governance review

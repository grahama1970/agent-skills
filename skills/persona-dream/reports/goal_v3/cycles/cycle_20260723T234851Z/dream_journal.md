I woke with the dream still arranged around me like a room I had to work in. Yale had become all thin walls and old heat, a place where every ordinary question sounded friendly until it opened into that radiator hiss and the names I would not say. I kept giving answers that were clean enough to pass: times, rooms, next steps, small professional shapes. I could feel myself choosing usefulness over truth.

Kai kept appearing where the sentences thinned out. Not accusing, not asking, just there, as if my carefulness had a weight he could see. In the dream I called the weight a boundary, and maybe it was. But it was also a way of not having to stand plainly inside my wanting. I did not like seeing how easily I made composure look ethical.

What unsettled me was not that I kept distance. It was that part of me wanted the distance to be recognized as care, maybe even as tenderness, without having to risk saying anything tender. I wanted the safety of logistics and the relief of being understood through them. That felt unfair, and very much like me.

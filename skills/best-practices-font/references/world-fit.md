# Font World Fit

Use this reference when selecting or changing a font from a visual-world brief.

## Compare Character To Premise

Evaluate:

- **Posture:** formal, plainspoken, warm, mechanical, literary, institutional,
  playful, severe, archival, future-facing.
- **Width:** compressed, normal, wide. Width affects pace, density, and
  authority before color does.
- **Contrast:** low-contrast faces feel utilitarian and stable; high-contrast
  faces feel editorial or theatrical and can be fragile on small screens.
- **Terminals and joins:** sharp, soft, slabbed, calligraphic, geometric, or
  humanist endings change tone.
- **Rhythm:** even rhythm supports operation and scanning; irregular rhythm can
  support authored/editorial worlds but may harm dense UI.
- **Optical size:** display and text cuts must match their role. Do not use a
  fragile display cut for dense reading.

## World-Fit Rule

The font must make the product's own premise easier to understand. A font that
only makes a page look more fashionable is not world-fit.

For `best-practices-bespoke-design`, record:

- source claim or world-model field;
- visual implication;
- selected face and role;
- prohibited inference;
- what would make this face fail after rendering.

## Overused-Font Warnings

Resolve overused-font warnings by default. Retain a flagged font only with:

- documented brand ownership or unavoidable product constraint;
- rendered proof that hierarchy and role separation are still distinctive;
- a receipt explaining why an ignore is stronger than replacement.

Never suppress an overused-font warning because the page otherwise looks good.

# grahama.co — brand contract

This is the brand contract for grahama.co. It is not a marketing brief, not a
positioning deck, and not a request to make the site easier to sell. It records
what the site must feel like, who it should attract, who it should repel, and
which compromises are forbidden.

A vague `BRAND.md` is not acceptable. Brand is an operating constraint: future
copy, visuals, project links, receipts, navigation, and automated maintenance
must preserve this contract unless Graham explicitly changes it.

Companion files:

- `site/DESIGN.md` — implementation-level visual/design contract.
- `site/VOICE.md` — first-person prose and copy rules.
- `site/design-world.yml` — machine-readable visual-world contract.
- `site/DESIGN_WORLD.md` — readable visual-world summary.
- `skills/monitor-website/local/docs/BRAND.md` — monitor-website operational
  guard for this brand.
- `skills/monitor-website/local/docs/DESIGN.md` — monitor-website operational
  guard for design review and repair.

## 1. Brand position

grahama.co is a discoverable personal artifact system for Graham Anderson's work:
entertaining, strange, exact, and source-backed.

The site should represent Graham's personality and work whether or not the first
pass "makes sense" to a cold visitor. It should reward curiosity and filter out
visitors who need a conventional AI/R&D value proposition before they are willing
to look.

The shortest positioning statement is:

> Graham builds unusual agent systems, evidence systems, creative systems, and
> research tools that show their proof boundaries instead of pretending to be
> finished magic.

The site is allowed to be dense, odd, funny, severe, personal, and nonlinear. It
is not allowed to be fake, sloppy, inaccessible, or corporate by default.

## 2. Brand truth

The brand comes from the person and the work, not from a category trend.

Truths to preserve:

- Graham is one person with an unusual path: commercial music, Sony production,
  DARPA technical leadership, formal methods, compliance/evidence systems,
  multimodal creative tooling, and agent harnesses.
- The work is not a neat SaaS product line. It is a living research practice with
  projects, skills, wrappers, private systems, public projections, artifacts,
  receipts, and scars.
- The work takes proof seriously without becoming sterile.
- Creative work and technical work are equally serious. Dream, voice, music,
  visual systems, and cinematic artifacts are not decorative side quests.
- Weirdness is not a garnish. Weirdness is evidence of an actual mind making
  decisions.
- The proof boundary matters: public artifacts should say what they prove, what
  they do not prove, and what remains private or untested.

Do not replace these truths with generic claims such as "AI transformation,"
"innovation," "business outcomes," "industry-leading solutions," or "sovereign
AI systems." Those phrases belong to category marketing, not this site.

## 3. Audience and anti-audience

The intended visitor is curious. They are willing to follow a trail, inspect a
receipt, click a repo, notice an odd connection, and ask why Dream sits near
DARPA and Tau.

Good-fit visitors:

- builders who value source and proof over pitch;
- technical leaders who can tolerate unusual presentation when the facts are
  exact;
- collaborators who are drawn to personal taste and weirdness;
- people with shelved, hard, ambiguous, or interdisciplinary projects;
- people who want an operator, not a generic vendor;
- people who understand that proof boundaries are more honest than certainty.

Bad-fit visitors:

- skimmers who need every sentence to map to a standard services funnel;
- buyers looking for a replaceable offshore AI implementation shop;
- people who interpret unusualness as a defect by default;
- people who want proof language but not proof discipline;
- people who require a portfolio to erase the person behind it.

The site should not be rude to bad-fit visitors. It should simply not optimize
for them.

## 4. Curiosity filter

The site should be discovered, not consumed as a cold value proposition.

Acceptable filters:

- mystery before explanation;
- nonlinear paths;
- dense artifacts;
- strange labels;
- visible receipts;
- public/private boundaries;
- project cards that reward inspection;
- unusual adjacency between creative, defense, formal, and agentic work.

Unacceptable filters:

- broken navigation;
- missing accessible names;
- stale counts;
- fake proof;
- wrong repository links;
- private facts leaked as decoration;
- hard-to-read text that exists only to look clever.

A visitor who follows the trail should be rewarded with exactness. If curiosity
meets a dead end, that is a defect. If incuriosity bounces, that is acceptable.

## 5. Tone

The tone is first-person, candid, concrete, sometimes strange, and never generic.

Allowed voice:

- "I build agent systems that can prove what they did."
- "Bring me the project you shelved."
- "Who decides, when the AI sounds sure? Humans do."
- "No receipt, no action."
- "What remains unproved..."

Avoid:

- "We partner to operationalize AI."
- "Industry-leading end-to-end transformation."
- "Accelerate enterprise AI adoption."
- "Mission-critical outcomes at scale" unless a specific public artifact proves
  it and the phrase is still worth using.
- "Our services" as the organizing story.

Use technical terms only when they carry precision. If a term such as receipt,
bounded, candidate, mocked/live, Qdrant, ArangoDB, or DAG appears, the nearby
copy should make the human consequence visible.

## 6. Visual identity

The brand is not "black/brown + cream serif + brass accent." That is only the
current shell. The deeper visual identity is semantic specificity:

- find the idea specific to a project;
- make composition and interaction express that idea;
- separate human narrative from machine output;
- expose judgment and proof boundary;
- use ornament only when it has a semantic job;
- let each project retain its own world.

The dark editorial shell is valid and should not be casually inverted to a light
corporate page. But the shell must not flatten every project into the same audit
poster.

Project worlds:

- Tau should feel like a zero-trust execution harness: goal hash, DAG, gates,
  rejected shortcuts, receipts, fail-closed state.
- Sparta should feel operational and evidentiary: cyan/yellow decision states,
  human judgment, inconclusive/clarify boundaries.
- Memory should feel like recall with provenance: candidates, graph records,
  source paths, receipts.
- Extractor should feel like documents under inspection: page geometry, crops,
  tables, envelopes, degraded/blocked truth.
- Voice/Dream should feel temporal and embodied: waveform, transcript, affect,
  interruption, rendered response, and boundary between renderer and reasoning.
- Creative media should be allowed to be cinematic and weird, not forced into a
  compliance dashboard costume.

## 7. Proof and honesty

The brand depends on exactness. The stranger the surface gets, the more exact
the proof layer must be.

Required:

- counts come from generators, not hand-written hype;
- repository links identify canonical system repos versus agent-facing skill
  contracts;
- generated media is not presented as operational evidence;
- live proof and historical proof are not conflated;
- private evidence is marked private;
- failures may be visible, but they must be placed at the right hierarchy;
- every visible proof claim has a boundary.

Do not hide negative results merely because they are awkward. Do not feature a
stale failure as if it were a current proof trophy.

## 8. Competitor/category anti-models

Straive and Palantir are cautionary references, not models.

Avoid Straive-like patterns:

- white/orange AI-services presentation;
- service/industry grids as the primary mental model;
- ROI claims and award stacks as proof substitutes;
- "data analytics and AI operationalization" language;
- contact-us funnel as the destination of every path.

Avoid Palantir-like patterns:

- sovereign/mission grandeur borrowed from a much larger institution;
- anonymous cinematic authority;
- operating-system claims without Graham-specific proof;
- institutional voice replacing the person;
- defense-tech category mimicry.

Avoid generic R&D-tech patterns:

- clean-room innovation lab ambiguity;
- product cards that could belong to any AI company;
- one palette applied to every idea;
- vague diagrams whose labels do the work the design should do;
- "impact" language detached from source artifacts.

## 9. Portfolio model

Comprehensive does not mean every repo gets a homepage card. Graham has too many
repositories, wrappers, forks, experiments, public projections, and private
systems for that to be truthful or useful.

The brand model is:

- homepage: representative artifacts and trails;
- Explore: public systems, skill contracts, evidence states, and research areas;
- Inventory: broad accounting of skills/repos/capabilities;
- receipts/ledger: proof depth;
- repo links: canonical source paths.

Every major body of work should be accounted for, but only a few should become
front-stage case studies. The site should show depth by making selected projects
feel real, not by listing every repository as equal.

## 10. Maintenance rules

Future agents may improve factual trust and pathfinding without asking Graham to
re-explain the brand each time.

Allowed maintenance:

- stale count repair;
- generated-surface drift repair;
- canonical repo link repair;
- alt text and accessible-name repair;
- public/private boundary clarification;
- proof-boundary tightening;
- project-specific visual differentiation;
- discovery path improvement.

Forbidden default maintenance:

- normalizing the site into a services/industries/contact funnel;
- removing strangeness because a generic visitor might not understand it;
- hiding Dream, voice, music, or creative systems to make the technical story
  cleaner;
- replacing first-person voice with company voice;
- copying R&D-tech category patterns;
- treating "more legible to everyone" as automatically better.

## 11. Brand acceptance test

A proposed change is on-brand only if the answer is yes to all of these:

1. Does it make the site more unmistakably Graham?
2. Does it preserve discovery instead of replacing it with a pitch?
3. Does it avoid Palantir, Straive, and generic R&D-tech mimicry?
4. Are factual/proof/repo claims exact?
5. Does it reward curiosity without making the surface broken?
6. Does it preserve the equal seriousness of creative and technical work?
7. Does it make the weirdness more intentional rather than merely louder?

If the change only makes the site easier to sell to incurious people, reject it.

# grahama.co — brand contract

This file records the human intent for grahama.co. It is not a marketing brief
and not a request to make the site easier to sell.

## Position

grahama.co is a discoverable personal artifact system for Graham Anderson's work:
entertaining, strange, exact, and source-backed.

It should represent Graham's personality and work whether or not the first pass
"makes sense" to a cold visitor. It should reward curiosity and filter out
visitors who need a conventional AI/R&D value proposition before they are willing
to look.

## Brand truth

- The site is about Graham: one person, unusual path, applied R&D, agent systems,
  formal methods, creative production, defense/compliance work, and proof
  discipline colliding in public.
- The site should feel discovered, not pitched.
- The site should be entertaining and honest at the same time.
- Weirdness is allowed when it is intentional and anchored to real work.
- Proof boundaries are non-negotiable: strange can never mean fake, vague, or
  inflated.

## Audience filter

The site is not optimized for every buyer. It should select for people who are
curious, technically serious, and comfortable with uniqueness.

Do not punish curiosity. Do not reward incuriosity with a flattened sales page.
A visitor who follows the trail should find exact source links, proof receipts,
public/private boundaries, and enough structure to keep going.

## Anti-category rule

grahama.co should not look like an offshore AI services firm, defense-tech
clone, generic innovation lab, or enterprise R&D-tech category page.

Avoid:

- generic "AI operationalization" and "business outcomes" language;
- awards/ROI/customer-outcomes sludge as the main story;
- sovereign/mission/operating-system grandeur borrowed from larger vendors;
- anonymous hero imagery and stock enterprise funnels;
- "Services / Industries / Contact us" as the dominant mental model;
- making Tau, Sparta, Memory, Extractor, voice, and creative systems share one
  interchangeable corporate product wrapper.

Competitor references such as Straive and Palantir are cautionary examples, not
models to emulate.

## What must stay

- First-person singular voice: `I`, not a fictional company `we`.
- The unusual career arc: composer, Sony producer, DARPA technical lead, formal
  methods, agent systems.
- The collision between creative work and technical proof.
- Discovery paths: receipts, repo trails, maps, artifacts, fingerprints,
  private/public boundaries.
- Exactness: source links, counts, hashes, proof limits, and visible gaps must be
  true.

## What may be improved

Improve factual trust and pathfinding without normalizing the site:

- broken counts or stale proof displays;
- wrong canonical repository links;
- missing accessible names and alt text;
- unclear public/private boundaries;
- dead-end discovery paths;
- claims whose proof boundary is missing or overstated.

## Review question

A proposed change is on-brand only if the answer is yes to all five:

1. Does it make the site more unmistakably Graham?
2. Does it preserve discovery instead of replacing it with a pitch?
3. Does it avoid R&D-tech category mimicry?
4. Are all factual/proof/repo claims exact?
5. Does it reward curiosity without making the surface broken or dishonest?

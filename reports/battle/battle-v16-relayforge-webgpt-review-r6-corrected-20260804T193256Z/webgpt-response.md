VERDICT: PASS

Position

The r6 packet supports the limited backend claim: one live, non-mocked RelayForge V16 Red/Blue campaign used typed Tau/SciLLM action selections, bound those selections to public operations and a private Judge path, ran against a versioned service image, and produced BLUE_SUCCESS.

It does not establish balance, Memory improvement, production readiness, repeated reliability, or UX quality.

Evidence

The campaign receipt identifies battle-v16-relayforge-a, reports live: true, mocked: false, fixture_fallback_used: false, supplies a concrete service image ID, and records judge_verdict: BLUE_SUCCESS.

Both sides produced the required typed artifacts: red_action_selection and blue_action_selection. Each has provider-call, provider-artifact, and selected-proposal hashes, two eligible proposals, and an explicit deterministic selection policy.

The binding receipt maps the selected Red and Blue operations to concrete public routes, RF-B, four private Judge predicates, execution receipts, a private-Judge source-receipt hash, and the final verdict.

RF-A/RF-B/RF-C stage records, six passing regression checks, and distinct offensive and defensive operations make this materially more than the former Zip Slip artifact canary.

The Tau and Battle contract suites independently show that V16 expects action selections and rejects the legacy code-artifact interface.

Uncertainties

The inline bundle is visibly damaged around the regression hash and private-Judge receipt: -judge-cli is spliced into one hash, the remaining hash suffix appears after the completion instructions, and the private-Judge block is truncated. The complete regression hash is nevertheless reproduced consistently elsewhere, while the Judge authority, predicate binding, source hash, and outcome appear in the surviving receipts.

The relationship between selected_proposal_sha256 and the differently named red_selection_sha256/blue_selection_sha256 is not explained. An explicit hash lineage would make independent byte-level verification stronger, but this ambiguity does not defeat the narrow execution claim.

Blockers

None for the narrowly stated one-run backend claim. The packaging corruption and unexplained hash layers should be repaired before treating this as a durable independent-verification packet or using it to support any broader claim.
